## Data-driven findings

1. Crackers Q activation is 0% throughout mF=0.8. At mF=1.0 it falls from 2.5% at a=1.0 to 0.0% at a=0.8; at mF=1.2 it falls from 16.0% to 0.0%.
2. The retention effect is therefore conditional on F economics: it is absent when F is favorable (mF=0.8), modest at mF=1.0, and strongest at mF=1.2. Results at a=0.9 and a=0.8 are nearly identical because Crackers Q is already inactive after the first retention reduction.
3. Crackers F activation changes only slightly: a=1.0 to a=0.8 changes it by 0.0, 0.5, and 2.5 percentage points at mF=0.8, 1.0, and 1.2. The evidence supports partial effectiveness-driven Q-F substitution, not a one-for-one within-item switch.
4. Mixed Q-F portfolio rates are 0% in all a=1.0 cells and become 1.5% and 4.0% at mF=1.0 and 1.2 after retention falls. Across the full panel, all 22 mixed portfolios are cross-item specializations: same-item coexistence=0, cross-item specialization=22.
5. At mF=1.2, a=1.0 to a=0.8 raises Water Q/F activation by 2.5/3.5 points and Vaccine Q/F activation by 12.0/2.5 points. Reallocation therefore extends beyond Crackers F to Water and especially Vaccine.
6. The mean T-COST increase from a=1.0 to a=0.8 is 0.000, 22320.158, and 131539.522 across mF=0.8, 1.0, and 1.2, versus 24758408.407 for mF=0.8 to 1.2 at a=1.0. F economics has the much larger T-COST effect.
7. Paid-R adoption is essentially stable across retention levels; its largest retention-linked change is 0.5 percentage points at mF=1.2. Reliability is therefore not the primary destination of the displaced Crackers-Q allocation in this panel.
8. The interaction is visible in activation and portfolio structure but is threshold-like rather than smooth: retention matters only when F economics is not already favorable, and the additional reduction from a=0.9 to a=0.8 adds almost no response.
